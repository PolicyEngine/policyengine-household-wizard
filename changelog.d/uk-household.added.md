Add a UK household model, `policyengine-household-wizard/uk-household`, with its request adapter at `policyengine-household-wizard/uk-household/adapters`. It is the household the UK Autumn Budget 2026 dashboard asks about, so apps that share it describe a UK household the same way. `UKHouseholdDraft` holds the answering adult, an optional partner whose answers are kept while switched off, children with their education from 16, region, tenure and rent, Council Tax band, Universal Credit, childcare and spending. `createTypicalDraft()` gives the dashboard's starting answers, and `UK_DEFAULT_SOURCES` gives the ONS releases behind its salary, rents and spending. `validate()` checks only what the calculation reads, against the household impact API's bounds and policy years (`UK_HOUSEHOLD_LIMITS`, `UK_POLICY_YEARS`), including child ages from 0 to 19 and State Pension amounts from State Pension age in the policy year (`UK_STATE_PENSION`, policyengine-uk 2.132.6, which an app can replace with its own table). `serializeDraft` and `deserializeDraft` round-trip every draft through URL parameters (a private pension or electricity left out comes back as 0 or `null`, which mean the same). `toHouseholdImpactRequest` builds the dashboard's `POST /api/household-impact` body; for each of 32 dashboard households (its examples, starting answers and form edge cases) it gives the request the dashboard's own code built, byte for byte, and property tests compare it with a copy of that code for every draft its form can hold, and check that every draft `validate()` accepts builds a request the dashboard's own request models accept, when sent with at least one measure they know. `ValidationIssue` and `ValidationResult` now come from one shared module; their shape is unchanged.
